Employer Sponsored Visa Australia: How the Process Really Works

Published October 2026 | Category: Work Visa Planning

Work Visa Planning

For many skilled workers, a job offer is only half the story. If the job is in Australia and you are not an Australian citizen or permanent resident, the employer often has to play an active part in the visa process. That is what makes employer sponsorship different from visas you apply for entirely on your own. Two parties are involved, each with their own obligations, and a problem on either side can stall the application.

This guide explains how the employer sponsored visa Australia system works in practice. It follows the process step by step from both the worker's and the employer's side, and it highlights where mistakes tend to happen.

The Big Picture: Who Does What

In a sponsorship arrangement, three stages usually happen, often in this order:

  1. The employer becomes an approved sponsor. The business applies to be a Standard Business Sponsor and shows that it is a genuine, operating business.
  2. The employer nominates a position. The nomination describes the job, the occupation, the salary and the reason an overseas worker is needed.
  3. The worker applies for the visa. The applicant shows they have the skills, experience, English level, health and character required for the visa.

Each stage has its own forms, fees and evidence. Applicants often focus only on stage three, but delays commonly start at stages one and two.

The Main Pathways

Australia offers several employer-sponsored options. Names and rules change, so always check the current list, but the main categories include:

Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482). A temporary visa that replaced the earlier Temporary Skill Shortage visa. It has different streams, such as Specialist Skills, Core Skills and Labour Agreement, with different salary and occupation requirements.

Employer Nomination Scheme (subclass 186). A permanent visa that allows an employer to nominate a worker for permanent residence. Pathways include moving from a temporary visa after working for the sponsoring employer, and a direct entry option in some cases.

Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional visa (subclass 494). A provisional regional visa that may lead to permanent residence. Regional definitions matter here, and Perth has been treated as a designated regional area for some visa purposes. Confirm the current rules rather than assuming.

Your best option depends on your occupation, experience, age, English level and the employer's situation. This is where a qualified Australia work visa consultant can save time, because the right pathway is not always the obvious one.

Step One: The Employer's Sponsorship Approval

Before nominating anyone, an employer usually needs sponsorship approval. Typical expectations include:

  • A lawfully operating business, with registration and financial records
  • Evidence of genuine business activity
  • A commitment to meet sponsor obligations
  • Payment of applicable fees and, in many cases, a training contribution such as the Skilling Australians Fund levy

Smaller employers sometimes assume they are too small to sponsor. That is not necessarily true, but they must be able to show genuine trading activity and the capacity to employ and pay the worker.

Step Two: Nominating the Position

The nomination is where the job is tested against the rules. Common points of scrutiny:

  • Occupation. The role must match an occupation that is eligible for the visa. A job title alone is not enough; the actual duties must match the occupation description.
  • Salary. The pay must meet the relevant salary threshold and the market rate for that role. Paying below market is a frequent cause of refusal and can affect the sponsor's standing.
  • Genuine position. The job must be real and needed by the business, not created only to support a visa.
  • Labour market testing. For some visa streams, the employer may need to show it advertised the role locally before turning to overseas candidates. Rules on this change, so check current requirements.

Employers who prepare a clear position description, accurate salary details and supporting business evidence tend to avoid delays here.

Step Three: The Worker's Application

Once a nomination exists or is lodged with it, the worker applies. Expect to provide:

  • Identity documents, including your passport
  • Skills evidence, such as qualifications, employment references and sometimes a formal skills assessment
  • English language evidence, if required for your visa and occupation
  • Health and character documents, such as medical examinations and police certificates
  • Information about dependants, if family members are included

Keep your work history consistent. Dates, job titles and duties in your résumé, references and forms should line up. Inconsistent details are among the most common reasons for requests for further information.

Obligations After Approval

Sponsorship does not end when the visa is granted. Employers carry continuing duties, including:

  • Paying at least the agreed salary and meeting equal employment terms
  • Keeping records of the sponsored worker's employment
  • Notifying the department of certain changes, such as the worker leaving or the business changing
  • Cooperating with compliance checks
  • Following rules about who pays for what, since sponsors cannot generally pass certain sponsorship costs on to the worker

Workers have responsibilities too. You must work in the nominated occupation, comply with visa conditions and tell the department about important changes. If your employment ends, there are time limits for finding a new sponsor or taking other steps, so do not leave this unattended.

Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Paying for a job offer. Any arrangement where a worker pays an employer or agent for a sponsorship or job offer can be unlawful and can lead to serious consequences for everyone involved.
  • Choosing the wrong occupation. Picking an occupation that sounds close but does not match your duties can lead to refusal.
  • Ignoring salary rules. Even a small shortfall can be a problem.
  • Providing unverifiable experience. Exaggerated or unsupported work history is a serious risk.
  • Assuming permanent residence is automatic. Many sponsored visas have conditions and pathways, but each has its own eligibility requirements.
  • Using unregistered advisers. Only registered professionals can legally provide immigration assistance in Australia.

Choosing the Right Adviser

In Australia, immigration assistance is regulated. Registered migration agents appear on a public register, and Australian legal practitioners can also provide immigration assistance. Before engaging anyone, ask for their registration number and check it yourself.

A good visa consultant Australia applicants can trust will:

  • Review your profile and explain realistic options
  • Provide a written service agreement and fee breakdown
  • Be clear about what they can and cannot control
  • Never guarantee a visa outcome
  • Give you copies of everything lodged

If you are applying from Western Australia or planning to work there, an Australia immigration consultant in Perth can also explain state-related considerations, such as local employer needs and the way regional rules may apply. Local knowledge helps, but it should always sit alongside verified registration and clear written terms.

A Realistic Timeline

Processing times vary by visa, occupation and how complete the application is, so check official estimates rather than relying on old figures. As a rule of thumb, build in time for:

  • Employer sponsorship approval and nomination
  • Skills assessment or English testing if needed
  • Health and character checks
  • Any additional information requests

Do not resign from your current job, sell property or book non-refundable travel until the visa is granted.

Where Visitor Visas Fit In

Some workers consider travelling to Australia on a visitor visa to look for a job in person. A visitor visa generally does not permit work, and employer sponsorship usually needs to be arranged separately. If you plan a short trip for interviews or meetings, speak to an Australia visitor visa consultant about what activities are allowed under your visa and what conditions apply. It is far safer to understand the limits before you travel.

Final Thoughts

Employer sponsorship works best when both parties prepare carefully. The employer needs a genuine business, a real role and a willingness to meet obligations. The worker needs accurate evidence and realistic expectations. Both benefit from clear advice from a properly registered professional.
